Question:
What is SNAP in Massachusetts?

Answer:
SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) helps eligible households buy food using an EBT card. In Massachusetts, SNAP is administered by the **Department of Transitional Assistance (DTA)**. Applying, interviews, and sending verifications are handled through DTA processes. This assistant can explain general program information and, after identity verification, help with **your** case using approved backend tools—not official determinations by itself.

Question:
How do I apply for SNAP or work on an application I already started?

Answer:
You can apply for SNAP through DTA. Many residents use **DTA Connect** (the Commonwealth online portal) to apply, upload documents, and complete case tasks. You may also contact your local DTA office for help. Typical steps include submitting an application, completing an interview if required, and providing **verifications** (such as income, identity, and housing costs) by the deadlines DTA gives you. If you already started an application, sign in to DTA Connect or call DTA to continue—have your name and date of birth ready when asking about **your** case.

Definition:
What are BEACON and DTA Connect?

Answer:
**BEACON** is Massachusetts DTA’s eligibility and case system used for SNAP and related programs. It is a state system name—not the same as private health plans that use the word “Beacon.” **DTA Connect** is the resident-facing online portal where you can apply, view tasks, and submit documents. Case status and missing verifications you hear about in a verified conversation reflect data shaped like BEACON in this demonstration environment.

Question:
What counts as proof of rent for SNAP verifications?

Answer:
For recertification or ongoing SNAP verifications, DTA may ask for **proof of current rent**. You can usually submit **one** document that shows your name, address, amount paid, and the period covered, such as: a signed **lease** showing monthly rent; a **rent receipt** for a recent month; a **canceled check** or bank statement showing payment to your landlord; or a **signed, dated landlord letter** on letterhead stating monthly rent and that you live at the address. The address should match your SNAP case. Upload through **DTA Connect** or mail to DTA—do not email sensitive documents to unverified channels.

Issue:
My rent increased but I do not have a new lease yet. What should I do?

Answer:
Tell DTA what changed and submit what you have. A **landlord letter** stating the new monthly rent amount may be accepted while you wait for a new lease. DTA staff review complex rent changes; this assistant cannot decide how a rent change affects your benefits. If your situation involves a dispute, a large increase, or you need a worker to review your file, ask to **speak with a DTA representative**—the assistant can connect you to a human agent in this demonstration.

Question:
Can this assistant tell me if I am approved for MassHealth?

Answer:
No. This assistant can conduct a **screening conversation** and explain possible next steps (such as starting an application). Only the **MassHealth agency** makes an **official eligibility determination** after a complete application and verification. Screening language uses phrases like “you may qualify” or “you may be eligible to apply”—never “approved” or “denied” based on screening alone.
